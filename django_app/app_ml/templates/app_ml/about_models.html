Meet the Models 🧠 How we trained our ML brains

This page explains how the image classifier (dog/cat/bird) and the Steam review sentiment model were trained: datasets, architectures, hyperparameters and why these configurations were chosen.

Dog · Cat · Bird CNN 🐶🐱🕊️
01 · Image model

Convolutional Neural Network trained to classify images into three classes: dog, cat and bird. The training process is documented in the notebook 01_cnn_animales_imagenes.ipynb.

Dataset & preprocessing

  • Images collected from Kaggle dog/cat and bird datasets and merged into three classes.
  • Balanced dataset per class: about 3507 images per class for train, 751 for validation and 753 for test.
  • Train/Val/Test split ≈ 70% / 15% / 15%.
  • Images resized to 128×128 pixels and normalized to [0, 1].
  • Data augmentation in training: small rotations, shifts, zoom and horizontal flips.

Architectures compared

  • Model 1 – Simple CNN: 2 Conv2D+MaxPooling blocks, dense layer with 128 units and dropout 0.5.
  • Model 2 – Deeper CNN: 3 Conv blocks with more filters (32, 64, 128), dense layer with 256 units and dropout 0.5.

Hyperparameter search

  • Optimizers tested: Adam and SGD with different learning rates.
  • Loss functions: CategoricalCrossentropy and the same loss with label smoothing.
  • The best configuration was: Model 2 + Adam (lr = 5e-4) + CategoricalCrossentropy trained for 15 epochs.

Results (test set)

  • Test accuracy ≈ 0.85 (85%).
  • Very good recall for birds, and balanced performance on dogs and cats.
  • The confusion matrix and classification report show that the deeper CNN generalizes better than the small CNN and than settings with higher learning rates.
Steam Review LSTM 🎮
02 · Text model

LSTM-based model trained on the Kaggle dataset Sentiment Analysis for Steam Reviews to classify reviews as recommended or not recommended. The full experiment is in 02_rnn_lstm_steam.ipynb.

Dataset & preprocessing

  • Source CSV: train.csv with columns user_review and user_suggestion.
  • Labels: 1 → positivo (recomendado), 0 → negativo (no recomendado).
  • Basic cleaning: lowercase, removal of URLs, non-alphabetic characters cleanup and space normalization.
  • Split into Train / Val / Test with proportions ≈ 70% / 15% / 15% (stratified).
  • Tokenizer with vocab size up to 30k tokens, sequences padded to 200 tokens.

Models compared

  • Simple RNN: Embedding(128) + SimpleRNN(64) + Dense(1, sigmoide).
  • LSTM 1: Embedding(64) + LSTM(64) + Dense(1, sigmoide).
  • LSTM 2 (final): Embedding(128) + LSTM(128) with dropout=0.3 and recurrent_dropout=0.3 + Dense(1, sigmoide).

Training setup

  • Loss: BinaryCrossentropy with slight label smoothing (0.05).
  • Optimizer: Adam with different learning rates per configuration.
  • Batch size: 64.
  • Final best model (LSTM 2): lr = 1e-4, 10 epochs.

Results & model selection

  • Best validation accuracy: LSTM 2 reached ≈ 0.78, clearly higher than Simple RNN and LSTM 1.
  • Test accuracy of the chosen model (LSTM 2): ≈ 0.77.
  • Confusion matrix shows a good balance between positive and negative reviews.
  • For this reason, the web app loads the LSTM 2 checkpoint as the final model.
